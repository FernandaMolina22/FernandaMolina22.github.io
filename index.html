MSc Thesis · Remote Sensing + ML2026

Topology-Aware Road Extraction from Sentinel-2 Imagery

A controlled experiment on whether topology-aware auxiliary supervision changes how a U-Net learns road segmentation from satellite imagery.

Problem

Road masks can score well pixel-wise while still breaking important network continuity.

Approach

Compared a single-output U-Net with multi-task variants using centerline and junction supervision during training.

Evaluation

Prepared the dataset, trained and validated controlled variants, and compared pixel-wise plus connectivity and fragmentation behaviour.

Main takeawayThe thesis focused on learning behaviour rather than only chasing a better headline metric, revealing precision–recall trade-offs and useful qualitative recovery effects from topology-aware supervision.
Technical detail

The research objective was not to build the most complex road-segmentation architecture. It was to isolate how explicit topology-aware supervision affects the learning process of a controlled U-Net setup. The final evaluation focused on the binary road mask, while centerline and junction branches acted as training signals.
